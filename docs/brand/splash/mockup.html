Ferrix boot splash

Mockup. One idea: the slash is the progress. The mark stands still in grey; its rust stroke starts unlit and fills from the foot up as the boot passes real milestones. No spinner, no bar, no text unless something needs saying. Click any screen to see it full size.

Storyboard

Six frames of one boot. The mark is drawn once, by the loader, and is not redrawn until the desktop takes the screen over; only the slash changes.

Variants

A is the proposal. B adds the word for machines where the name is not known from context (a first boot, a demo). C is the conventional answer, shown for comparison.

When something needs saying

The screen stays silent while the boot is on time. Text appears only in these cases, in one line under the mark.

Other screens

The mark is one fifth of the screen's shorter side tall and centred, on any size and orientation. The slash still reads at 480 pixels.

What makes it buildable

  • Two flat polygons on one flat colour (#0d0f12). No gradient, no alpha, no font on the default path: the loader can draw it into the firmware's framebuffer with a scanline fill.
  • A progress step repaints only the slash's rows that changed: a few thousand pixels, no frame clock, nothing to animate while the kernel has interrupts off.
  • Progress comes from milestones, not from time: loader, kernel, drivers, root, services, desktop. A slow stage shows as a slash that waits, which is true.
  • The desktop's first frame starts from the same picture and moves the mark to its place on the wallpaper, so there is no black flash between the two.
