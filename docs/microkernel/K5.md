## 1. What there is today

The Linux `futex(2)` and `futex_waitv(2)` are `src/kernel/src/syscall/futex.rs`
(1,008 lines). That file is in the **load** ring
(`tools/common/data/certification-item.json`). Most of it is not Linux at all.
It is a generic mechanism under a thin Linux decoder:

| Part | Lines (about) | Linux-specific? |
|---|---|---|
| `Key` (private: space and address; shared: `Arc<Vmo>` and offset), `Key::bucket` | 70 | no |
| `TABLE`, 256 buckets each with its own `SpinLock<Vec<Entry>>`; `Sleeper`, `Entry`, `rouse` | 150 | no |
| `wait_on`/`list`/`unlist`: read outside the lock (faulting), read again under the bucket lock with `copy_from_user_present`, list; sleep; unlist under the lock | 140 | no |
| `rouse` (wake N, bitset, oldest first, stale vector entries not counted), `requeue` (two buckets, lower first, room reserved before anything moves) | 170 | no |
| self-check hooks (`waiters_on`, `forget_waiters`, `list_stale_entry`, `bucket_of`, `requeue_without_moving`) | 120 | no |
| `sys_futex`, `sys_futex_waitv`: op decoding, `FUTEX_PRIVATE_FLAG`, relative vs absolute timespecs, `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME`, `count <= 0` wakes one, `ERESTARTSYS`, the `futex_waitv` vector layout and its i386 rule | 200 | **yes** |
| `wake_address` (the `CLONE_CHILD_CLEARTID` wake at a thread's end) | 10 | caller is Linux |

Its checks are in `syscall/check.rs` (load): `check_futexes`,
`check_futex_waitv`, the requeue checks, `a_shared_futex_crosses_a_fork`
(`Verifies: L.user.78`, whose unit `user::space::AddressSpace::shared_object_at`
is already core), and their negative controls.

The native ABI (`src/lib/proto/native-abi/src/nr.rs`) has no such call.
`ObjectWaitOne` (0x1008) waits on an object's signals, not on memory.

## 2. The mechanism

**K5 lifts the generic part into the core and puts the native calls on it.
It does not write a second table.** One new core file, and the Linux
`futex` becomes a client of it:

* `src/kernel/src/user/address_wait.rs` (**core**, under `user/**`): `Key`,
  the table, `Sleeper`, `wait_on`, `wake`, `requeue`, the
  read-outside-then-present-under-lock rule, and the self-check hooks the
  checks need. It names nothing above the core. It answers its own error
  type, `WaitError { Changed, Fault, NoMemory, Interrupted, TimedOut }`, and
  never a Linux errno.
* `src/kernel/src/syscall/native.rs` (**item**): four native calls that
  decode registers and call it (§3).
* `src/kernel/src/syscall/futex.rs` (**load**) keeps only the Linux decoding:
  ops, flags, timespec widths, `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME`, `count <= 0` wakes
  one, `ERESTARTSYS` for a signal, `futex_waitv`'s vector. It calls
  `user::address_wait`, which is the allowed direction (load → core). Its
  observable behaviour is unchanged, and its checks in `syscall/check.rs`
  run unchanged. That is the regression gate for the move.

What the core function takes in place of today's Linux types:

* **Who may leave**: `interrupted: &dyn Fn() -> bool`. The Linux shim passes
  `process.wait_interrupted()`. `native.rs` passes `must_leave(caller)`, as
  `object_wait_one` does.
* **The deadline**: an absolute `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` nanosecond count
  (`timer::now_nanos`'s clock), or none. Relative timeouts and real-time
  conversion stay with the caller (§5).
* **The limit of a wake**: a `usize` taken literally. Zero wakes none. Linux's
  "zero or less wakes one" is the shim's rule.

### 2.1 Keys: private, and shared by VMO and offset

A wait is keyed in one of two ways. A wake finds exactly the waits of its key.

* **Private**: `(space, address)`, the caller's `AddressSpace` by identity and
  the word's user address. Two processes' words at one address are two words.
  The space cannot be freed and its address reused while a waiter is listed,
  because every waiter holds its process (as today).
* **Shared**: `(Arc<Vmo>, byte offset)`. The same word in every space that maps
  the VMO, at whatever address each maps it. A shared key is reached in two
  ways, and **both give the same key**:
  1. **By mapping**: the call sets `SHARED` and names a user address. The key
     is `AddressSpace::shared_object_at(address)` (the existing unit of
     `L.user.78`). Native `VMO_MAP` regions are always shared (`map_object`
     sets `shared: true`), so a native mapping of a VMO always gives a
     shared key. An address in a private region, or in none, gives a private
     key. That is Linux's rule, and K5 keeps it, so that a `linuxd` mapping
     Linux's calls onto K5 needs no rule of its own.
  2. **By handle**: the call sets `BY_VMO` and passes a VMO handle and a byte
     offset in place of an address. The word is read through the VMO
     (`Vmo::read_present` under the bucket lock, `Vmo::read_page` or
     `fill_one_page` outside it), never through any mapping. No mapping is
     needed. `linuxd` can wake a program's shared word after the program's
     restricted space is gone (a `CLONE_CHILD_CLEARTID` word in shared
     memory, a robust-list entry). The handle needs `READ`, for waits and
     wakes alike: a wake changes no memory and at most ends a wait early,
     which every caller of a wait must survive anyway (spurious wakes are
     part of the contract, §3).

A key holds its `Arc<Vmo>`, so a VMO's identity cannot be reused while
anything waits on it. A key is therefore never dropped under a bucket lock,
since the last reference may free pages (today's rule, kept).

### 2.2 No lost wake

Kept as today and stated as a requirement. The word is read, and the entry
listed, under the key's bucket lock. A waker takes the same lock to look. A
waker that changed the word before the read makes the read see the change.
One that changed it after finds the entry.

The read under the lock never faults. It is `copy_from_user_present` for a
mapping, or `Vmo::read_present` for a handle. If the page is not there, the
lock is let go, the page is faulted or filled in with nothing held, and the
read is done again. A word in a VMO page that was never committed and has no
source reads as zero. It stays uncommitted: `read_page`'s rule, so a wait
never commits memory.

Lock order: a bucket lock, then (handle-addressed only) the VMO's page lock,
a leaf. Nothing takes a bucket lock under a VMO lock or a space lock. Key
construction takes the space lock, so it happens before any bucket lock (as
today).

### 2.3 The deadline: one timer queue

K5 adds no timer and no queue. A waiter blocks through
`WaitQueue::wait_on_any` with its deadline, which files the task's sleep
deadline in its run queue's sleeper list (`Task::set_sleep_deadline`). That
is the scheduler's own timer queue, the one every wait in the kernel already
uses.

**K10's timer objects should file on the same queue.** The PO asked that the
two not have separate queues. That note goes to the K10 agent through the PO.
K5's part is only to add none.

The clock is `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` in nanoseconds, absolute, as
`ObjectWaitOne`'s. A deadline already past still lists and reads the word
once: a changed word answers `SHOULD_WAIT`, and an unchanged one answers
`TIMED_OUT` without sleeping. A woken waiter whose deadline has also passed
answers woken. A wake that took the entry off the table counted it, and
answering a timeout would lose that wake for whoever it was meant for (today's
rule).

### 2.4 What happens on unmap

* **A wait on an address no region maps**: the first read faults and the call
  answers `FAULT`. Nothing is listed.
* **The word's page unmapped while a waiter sleeps** (another thread's
  `munmap`, or a native unmap when there is one): the waiter keeps sleeping
  until a wake of its key, its deadline or its leaving. Its entry does not
  depend on the mapping. A shared key holds its VMO, so the VMO and its pages
  live until the waiter leaves, charged to whoever pays for them already. This
  is Linux's behaviour.
* **A wake by address after the unmap**: the waker's key is built from what
  is mapped now. A private key `(space, address)` still finds a private waiter
  at that address. A `SHARED` wake on an address no shared region holds gives
  a private key, and so does not find a shared waiter. A `BY_VMO` wake still
  finds the shared waiter: that is the route for a waker without the mapping.
* **Remapped elsewhere, or another VMO mapped at that address**: a shared key
  follows the VMO, not the address, so a wake through the new mapping finds
  only the new object's waiters.
* **The space torn down** (`exec`, exit): its threads are the only holders of
  private keys on it, and each leaves through the interrupted path first.
  Nothing outlives the space. Shared entries are other processes' and are
  unaffected.

### 2.5 Charging and bounds

**Charged.** Today a futex waiter is "argued through the task limit, not
charged" (`MEMORY-AND-TIMING.md` §1.6). Under K5 every allocation a wait makes
is fallible and charged to the calling task's job through `ferrix_kmem::Charge`
(F-37's account, `quota::charge_kernel`):

* the `Sleeper` with its per-slot bucket records;
* each `Entry`'s place in its bucket. A bucket's `Vec` grows through
  `ferrix_kmem::reserve` against the sleeper's charge, so growth slack is paid
  by the job that caused it;
* the copied vector of `ADDRESS_WAIT_ANY`, freed before the sleep.

A charge refused answers `NO_MEMORY`, with nothing listed. The charge is
released when the waiter leaves. A bucket's room that outlives its entries
(slack) stays charged to the sleeper that grew it until that sleeper leaves,
then becomes the machine's. The machine's slack is bounded by the largest
bucket ever seen, and the check measures it. If the consultant prefers, a
bucket shrinks back when it is a quarter full, as epoll's hook lists do.

**Walks under a spin lock**, each stated in `MEMORY-AND-TIMING.md` (a new
§2.2r, *The address-wait table*):

* **List**: one present-read of 4 bytes, plus one push into reserved room.
  O(1).
* **Unlist**: a linear search of one bucket for the sleeper's entry. O(n),
  n the entries in that bucket.
* **Wake N**: one bucket, linear, stopping after N counted. O(n) for the
  bucket.
* **Requeue**: two buckets' locks, lower index first. One pass to count what
  may move, and one to move. O(n_from + moved).

Here **n is bounded** by the entries listed machine-wide. Each task has at most
one wait at a time, of at most `WAIT_ANY_MAX` = 128 entries. Tasks are bounded
by `PID_MAX` (32,768) and by each job's task limit. So a bucket holds at most
128 × (tasks): a few million in the worst case, and only if every waiter's key
hashes to one bucket. The hash is a Fibonacci mix of the space's or VMO's
kernel address and the word's address or offset. A program does not know the
kernel addresses, so it cannot aim at one bucket, but that is an argument, not
a guarantee. **Question Q3 for the consultant**: should the bucket index also
take a boot-random key (`random.rs`), making the argument cryptographic, or
should a per-bucket cap answer `NO_MEMORY` past it? Either way the bound goes
in §2.2r with the boot check's measurement: the check lists 1,024 dormant
entries in one bucket with no threads (the stale-entry hook) and times one
wake walk over them. The figure per entry is recorded per architecture.

No walk allocates, sleeps or faults under a bucket lock.

## 3. The native calls

A new family in the native table. The numbers are **allotted by
the PO** (the table on `main` ends at `0x1065`; `0x1066`-`0x1067` are kept for
chardev):

| Number | Call | Registers | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0x1068` | `ADDRESS_WAIT` | `(address, expected: u32, bitset: u32, flags, deadline: *u64, vmo)` | 0 when woken |
| `0x1069` | `ADDRESS_WAKE` | `(address, count: u32, bitset: u32, flags, vmo)` | how many were woken |
| `0x106A` | `ADDRESS_REQUEUE` | `(from, to, wake: u32, move: u32, flags, expected: u32)` | how many were woken or moved |
| `0x106B` | `ADDRESS_WAIT_ANY` | `(words: *Word, count, flags, deadline: *u64)` | the index of the word whose wake took it |

`0x106C`-`0x106F` are kept for the family.

* **`flags`**: `SHARED` (bit 0), `BY_VMO` (bit 1; `address` is then a byte
  offset into the VMO named by `vmo`, and `SHARED` is implied), `COMPARE`
  (bit 2, `ADDRESS_REQUEUE` only: refuse with `SHOULD_WAIT` unless `from`
  holds `expected`, under both buckets' locks). Every other bit is refused
  with `INVALID_ARGS`. **Bit 3 is kept for K11**: "the address is in the
  calling thread's restricted space" (§5). It is refused until K11 defines it.
  **`BY_VMO` is refused on `ADDRESS_REQUEUE`** in K5. Requeue's six
  registers are full, leaving none for a handle, and its users (condition
  variables) requeue between words of one mapping. A handle-addressed requeue can be added in the family's room if
  `linuxd` needs one.
* **`Word`** (16 bytes, `ADDRESS_WAIT_ANY`): `address: u64, expected: u32,
  flags: u32` with per-word `SHARED` only. `count` is 1 to 128.
  `ADDRESS_WAIT_ANY` has no `BY_VMO` in K5 for the same reason, and no
  bitset, as `futex_waitv` has none.
* **`bitset`**: zero is refused with `INVALID_ARGS`. `0xFFFF_FFFF` matches
  every wait.
* **`count`**: woken literally, 0 wakes none, `u32::MAX` wakes all.
* **`deadline`**: a pointer to an absolute `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` nanosecond value,
  or null for none, exactly as `ObjectWaitOne`.
* **The word** must be 4-aligned (`INVALID_ARGS` otherwise). By address, it
  must be in the user range and readable (`FAULT`). By handle, the offset plus
  4 must lie inside the VMO (`INVALID_ARGS`). The handle must be a VMO
  (`WRONG_TYPE`), live (`BAD_HANDLE`), with `READ` (`ACCESS_DENIED`, which
  `native::dispatch` already audits as `audit::RIGHTS`).
* **Answers of a wait**: `SHOULD_WAIT` when the word does not hold `expected`
  (nothing listed). `TIMED_OUT`. `EINTR` when the caller must leave (as
  `ObjectWaitOne`). `NO_MEMORY` when a charge or allocation is refused.
  `FAULT`.
* **Spurious wakes are allowed**: a woken wait answers 0, and the caller
  re-reads its word, as every futex user does. The kernel never answers 0
  for a wait that was not woken, timed out or interrupted. But any process
  that can read the word (a mapping, or a handle with `READ`) can wake it.
  This is stated in the ABI documentation.
* **Vector state**: the two waits block, but are **not** added to the
  vector-state contract's three calls. They keep the whole vector state, as
  every other native call does, so `arch::*::destroys_vector_state` is
  unchanged. Joining the contract is a later optimisation with its own
  review.
* **seccomp, the fast path, `PORT_FD`**: none affected. The calls are
  answered by the item's `match`, not by `serve`, so nothing above the item
  registers for them.

The user-space library (`src/lib/proto/native`) gains `address_wait`,
`address_wake`, `address_requeue` and `address_wait_any` wrappers. They are
used by the boot check's user program, if one is needed (§6), and later by
`linuxd`.

## 5. How `futex(2)` sits on top (M5, not K5)

At M5 the Linux program runs in restricted mode (K1), and a `futex` call exits
to `linuxd`. `linuxd` maps it like this:

| Linux | K5 |
|---|---|
| `FUTEX_WAIT` (relative timeout) | `ADDRESS_WAIT`, `bitset = !0`, deadline = now + timeout |
| `FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET` (absolute, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`) | `ADDRESS_WAIT`, deadline as given |
| `… | FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME` | deadline = given − real-time offset, at the call (as today: a later clock step is not followed) |
| `FUTEX_PRIVATE_FLAG` absent | `SHARED` |
| `FUTEX_WAKE`, `FUTEX_WAKE_BITSET` | `ADDRESS_WAKE` with `max(count, 1)` |
| `FUTEX_REQUEUE`, `FUTEX_CMP_REQUEUE` | `ADDRESS_REQUEUE` (± `COMPARE`) |
| `futex_waitv` | `ADDRESS_WAIT_ANY` |
| `EINTR` from K5 | `ERESTARTSYS` if a signal is pending, `linuxd`'s signal logic |
| `CLONE_CHILD_CLEARTID` at a thread's end | `ADDRESS_WAKE`, `SHARED`, count 1, or `BY_VMO` once the space is gone |
| PI futexes, `FUTEX_WAKE_OP`, `FUTEX_LOCK_PI` | `ENOSYS`, as today. Nothing in K5 is promised for them |

The address is in the **program's restricted space**, not `linuxd`'s. Until
K11, the only space is the caller's own. K11 defines flag bit 3 ("the calling
thread's restricted space"), chosen by the kernel from the thread's mode, never
from an argument naming a space. This follows K11's two-table rule. The private
key is then `(restricted space, address)`, the same key a restricted program's
own native call would get if it ever made one.

## 6. The boot check and its negative controls

**`src/kernel/src/user/address_wait_check.rs`** (core), driven from a new
stage-9 slot `check_address_waits` after `check_native_objects`, in the
reference configuration (`present`). It builds processes as `object/check.rs`
does and drives every case through `native::dispatch` with raw registers:

1. **Wait then wake, private**: a kernel thread of process A waits on a word
   and is seen listed (`waiters_on`). A wakes it with count 1 and the answer
   is 1. The waiter answers 0.
2. **Changed word**: a wait on a word not holding `expected` answers
   `SHOULD_WAIT`, nothing listed.
3. **Shared across two processes, by mapping**: one VMO mapped into A and B at
   different addresses. A waits with `SHARED`, B wakes with `SHARED` and
   finds it. A private wake from B does not.
4. **Shared by handle**: A waits by mapping. A process with only a `READ`
   handle and no mapping wakes it `BY_VMO`. A handle without `READ` is refused
   `ACCESS_DENIED`.
5. **Wake N, oldest first, bitsets**: three waiters, a wake of 2 rouses the
   first two. A bitset sharing no bit rouses none.
6. **Requeue**: across two buckets (the existing bucket hook picks the words),
   then `COMPARE` refused on a changed word.
7. **Wait-any**: three words, a wake of the second answers index 1, and all
   three entries leave the table.
8. **Deadline**: a wait with a deadline 5 ms ahead answers `TIMED_OUT`, not
   before the deadline. A deadline already past answers at once.
9. **Interruption**: a waiter whose process is killed leaves with `EINTR`.
10. **Unmap**: a waiter sleeps while its word's mapping is unmapped by another
    thread, is still listed, and is woken `BY_VMO`. A wait on the unmapped
    address answers `FAULT`.
11. **Charged**: a job with its memory limit at its current use is refused
    `NO_MEMORY`, nothing listed, while a sibling job's wait succeeds.
12. **Refusals**: bad flags, a zero bitset, misalignment, `BY_VMO` out of
    range, a wrong type, a bad handle, `BY_VMO` on requeue and wait-any, and
    a count of 0 or 129 for wait-any.
13. **Walk measured**: 1,024 dormant entries in one bucket and one wake over
    them, timed and printed.

Its line (exact form at code time):
`  addrwait N waits woken across M processes, by mapping and by handle, K refusals as specified, walk W ns per entry`.

**Negative controls**, each a planted sabotage run through
`gate.sh control --expect`, each shown FIRED on every architecture it is
claimed for:

* **C1, lost wake**: `list` reads the word *before* taking the bucket lock
  (today's rule broken). Expected: case 1 or 3 times out, with the check's
  "a wake was lost" line.
* **C2, shared key by address**: `Key::new` keys shared waits by
  `(space, address)`. Expected: case 3 fails, "a shared waiter was not found
  across processes".
* **C3, handle key differs from mapping key**: `BY_VMO` builds a key on the
  handle's object with the offset rounded to the page. Expected: case 4 fails.
* **C4, wake counts without rousing**: the existing `forget_waiters` path used
  for the wake. Expected: case 1's waiter does not answer.
* **C5, requeue without moving**: the existing `requeue_without_moving`.
  Expected: case 6 fails.
* **C6, uncharged**: the sleeper's charge skipped. Expected: case 11 fails,
  "a wait beyond the job's limit was listed".
* **C7, the deadline ignored**: the deadline is not passed to the sleep.
  Expected: case 8 fails, "a wait outlived its deadline".

The existing Linux futex checks in `syscall/check.rs` and their controls run
unchanged on the moved code. That shows the move changed nothing for Linux
programs.

**Rows** (`cargo xtask gate-rows` names the final set): `xtask check` (the
traceability, fallible-allocation, unsafe and item-boundary steps), the
four boots (x86-64 under KVM and TCG, AArch64, ARMv7-A, at `--smp 2` for
ARMv7-A), and the rows that exercise Linux futexes hardest: `test-threads`,
the Wine/Proton esync and fsync row if it is in the table, and
`bench-chrome-video`'s contention case as a figure, not a gate.

## 7. Requirement ids (requested from the PO)

* **H.OBJ.20**: a wait on a word of user memory shall sleep only while the
  word holds the value its caller gave. It shall end when a wake of its key
  takes it, when its deadline passes, or when its caller must leave. A wake
  shall end at most the number of waits it names.
* **L.user.135-144**, unit `user::address_wait`:
  * 135: keys, private and shared (one key for a mapping and a handle on the
    same VMO byte);
  * 136: no lost wake (read and list under the bucket lock);
  * 137: the read under the lock never faults or commits;
  * 138: wake N, oldest first, bitset, stale entries not counted;
  * 139: requeue across buckets, compare under both locks, room before moves;
  * 140: wait-any (answer index, every entry unlisted);
  * 141: the deadline (monotonic, absolute, woken wins, never early);
  * 142: interruption;
  * 143: unmap (a listed wait survives, its key holds its VMO);
  * 144: charging (refused at the limit with nothing listed, released on
    leaving).
* **L.object.203-206**, unit `syscall::native::address_*`: the four calls'
  decoding and refusals (flags, bitset, alignment, the handle's type and
  right, the vector's count and layout).

### 10.1 Condition (5): buckets that grow and shrink outside their lock

The table keeps one `Vec<Entry>` per bucket, but **nothing is allocated or
freed under a bucket lock**, and every allocation is fallible:

* **Listing** pushes only into room the bucket already has. A full bucket's
  lock is let go, a vector of twice the room is allocated with nothing held,
  the lock taken again, the entries moved across (no allocation: the new
  room is there) and the old, now empty, vector freed after the lock. Then
  the word is read again and the push made. Refused memory is `NO_MEMORY`
  with nothing listed.
* **Leaving** shrinks: a bucket left holding fewer than a quarter of its
  room (and more than a minimum of 4) is swapped for one of half the room,
  allocated outside the lock, the old freed outside it.
* **A wake** takes entries off in rounds of at most 32 per lock hold into a
  fixed array on the stack, and drops them after the lock: no allocation, and
  the last reference to a key's VMO is never dropped under the lock. Each
  hold is bounded by the bucket's length plus 32 removals.
* **A requeue** counts what may move under both locks; if the target lacks
  the room, both locks go, the room is allocated outside, and the requeue
  starts again (re-reading the word for `COMPARE`). The keys it replaces go
  into a vector allocated the same way, dropped after both locks.

**Charged**: each waiter's `Sleeper` carries a `Charge` for its entries (one
`Entry` per word), the per-slot bucket records and the sleeper itself,
taken before anything is listed and given back when it leaves. **Uncharged**:
a bucket's slack, at most three times its live entries (the shrink rule)
plus the minimum room of 4 for each of the 256 buckets, so the machine holds
at most 3 x (live entries) x 48 bytes + 256 x 4 x 48 bytes (48 KiB) of
uncharged room. §2.2r states it and case 13 checks it after the 1,024-entry
walk drains.
