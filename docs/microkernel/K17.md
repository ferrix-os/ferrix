# K17: criticality levels on jobs

**Design, 2026-11-04 (mk-restricted, branch `feature/mk-restricted`). Reviewed: OK IF
(1)-(10), ledger 6080, folded in.** `docs/MICROKERNEL.md` §2.5 and §3, row K17, stage M3.

Ids: **H.OBJ.34** and native **0x10E0 `JOB_DECLARE_BUFFER`** (past every block, the review
having asked the PO for each, 2026-11-04; re-numbered if the PO prefers), **L.object.283-289**,
**L.user.185-188**; audit kinds `LEVEL` (Refused 8), `BUFFER_DECLARED` (Granted 9); panic code
**FX-0926** (`STAGE9_LEVELS`), one stage 9 line, `levels`.

## 1. What it is

Each job has a **level**, 0 (QM) to 4, fixed as it is made. Between jobs of one level
nothing here applies. The kernel refuses what would let a lower level reach into a
higher one, by the holder's job at the moment a handle would land in a table, and
audits each refusal. Everything on today's profiles is one level: a job without the
option takes its parent's, and the root job is 4, so nothing here refuses anything
until a partition manager asks for a lower level. No flag and no boot option chooses
it; it is data on jobs.

## 2. The interface

* `JOB_CREATE(parent, options)`: `options` gains `JOB_LEVEL_SET` (bit 9, bit 8 being K14's
  `JOB_MEASURED`) and `JOB_LEVEL_SHIFT` (bits 10 to 12, the level). Without the set bit, the
  parent's level. A level above the parent's is `ACCESS_DENIED`, audited; past 4 is
  `INVALID_ARGS`. `MANAGE` on the parent, as today.
* **`declared`** (K14's rules read it): a job is declared once it or an ancestor asked for
  a level, for good, and K14's "a level above QM implies measured" and "no restricted
  execution above QM" read declared levels alone, so that today's jobs, at the top by
  inheritance, are not caught by them. K17's own comparisons are numeric. **`linuxd`'s
  partition job sets level 0 explicitly**, and at M5 the manager's description names every
  level, with a check, since forgetting one yields 4.
* `JOB_DECLARE_BUFFER(job, vmo)`: declares a VMO a buffer that lower levels may write and
  `job`'s level may map (§2.5). `SET_LIMIT` on the job and `MAP` on the VMO, which must have no
  pager and be mapped at no level above; once; audited as a grant.
* A process moved between jobs of different levels is refused, audited by its caller; a
  process placed as it is made takes its job's level. **A region is one level**:
  `PROCESS_CREATE_SHARED` refuses a job of another level than the region's, audited, since
  its shared table crosses its processes' jobs.

## 3. The rules

The level of an object: a process's, thread's, job's and region's is their job's, kept
in the object (`Exit`, `ThreadRef`, `Job`, `Region`) because moves across levels are
refused so it never changes; a space's is its owner's job's, in `SpaceRef`.

**The one door.** `object::level::may_hold(holder: u8, object: &Object, rights) ->
Result<(), Refused>`, O(1) per handle up to `CHANNEL_MAX_HANDLES`, is asked wherever a handle
lands in a table of another job's: `deliver` (the attested read too; `channel_write_read`
carries no handles), `move_handle`, `give_bootstrap`, `process_start_shared`'s move. A handle a
job makes for itself cannot rise above it, but for `JOB_FOR_CGROUP`, which reaches an ancestor's
job: refused above the caller's level. K2's creator case is held by (e), since a creator's
job is at or above its child's; K13's keeper authority, when it lands, goes through this door. The
refusal on a read cannot be the read's failure, since that would let a higher level jam a lower
one's channel: **at final delivery alone the message is delivered with that handle's slot
`Handle::INVALID`, and the object is disposed and the refusal audited outside the lock**. A read
answered too small, or put back, refuses nothing. `ReadActual` is unchanged: the slot says
which. **The VMO's lock is a leaf**, in no other's order.

| Rule (§2.5) | How |
|---|---|
| (a) no handle to a higher job's processes, threads, jobs, spaces, region, or pager, since its holder fills what the job reads | `may_hold`: refused when the object's level is above the holder's. L.object.283 |
| (b) no write right to a VMO a higher job maps, but a declared buffer | each VMO keeps `lowest_writer`, its creator's level at first, and `highest_mapper`. A landing `WRITE` handle lowers the first; a map raises the second, by the **target space's** level, not the caller's; either is refused if it would leave `lowest_writer < highest_mapper` and the VMO is not declared for exactly that mapper's level. `SPACE_CLONE` and K11's bind map without a map call, so each is refused across levels. L.user.185, L.user.186 |
| (c) no right to change a higher job's budget or reservation | by (a): those rights are on the job's handle, of which a lower level holds none. L.object.283 names it |
| (d) no resize, decommit or range right, and never its pager | no native call gives a range right; `Rights` has none to refuse, and the check holds that (the table of calls, §5). A pager-backed VMO's level is its pager's job's; a map by a higher level is refused, and no declaration lifts it. L.user.187 |
| (d), the pager itself | a pager is at its making job's level, so its VMOs take that level whoever makes them, and its handle passes the door: whoever holds it fills what the job reads. A level-2 job holding a level-0 pager makes a VMO marked level 0 and is refused the map. L.user.187 |
| (e) a child's level is at most its parent's | `JOB_CREATE`. L.object.284 |
| (f) a speculation domain never spans levels, under `partition` too | a domain is one job (`Job::domain`, set as it is made, not inherited), and a move across levels is refused, so a domain has one level, under every flag value. L.object.285; the cache partitions of §2.5 wait for T1 |
| (g) every refusal audited | `audit::LEVEL`, subject the caller, target the object, the two levels in the words. L.object.286 |

**A kernel mapping of a VMO** (a device pin, a kernel view) is no job's and raises
nothing; pins go by `Rights` and K8 as they do.

**What K17 does not cover.** A channel between levels is allowed: masquerade is K6's,
flooding K7's, and corruption, repetition, loss and delay are the higher part's by
assumption of use (§2.5). The timing channels named there are K9's and T1's. Waiting:
"no higher part blocks on a lower one without a timeout" is an assumption of use, not
something the kernel can see.

## 5. Checks and controls

Stage 9's `levels` (`object/level_check.rs`), native calls from check processes as K4's
are. Jobs at 4, 2 and 0:

| Case | Control (one edit, FIRED in the commit) |
|---|---|
| A level-0 process reads a message carrying a level-2 process, thread, job, space and region handle: each slot invalid, the bytes delivered, audit count up; level-2 holder keeps them | `may_hold` answers yes |
| The negative of the row: a level-0 job offered a write mapping of a level-2 job's private VMO, refused; the same VMO read-only, allowed | the VMO's level test off |
| The other order: a level-0 job holds `WRITE` first, then level 2's map is refused | the map's test off |
| A declared buffer: level 0 writes, level 2 maps; a level-1 map of it refused, undeclared | a declaration lifts every level |
| A level-0 pager's VMO, made by level 2 holding that pager: a level-2 map refused, and not declarable. A level-2 pager's handle does not land at level 0 | the pager's test off; `set_pager_level` by the caller |
| A child above its parent refused; without the option the parent's level; `JOB_GET_QUOTA` reads it | the bound off |
| A domain job's child of another level refused | the test off |
| A move across levels refused | the test off |
| Each refusal's audit record | the record removed |
| An exception's handle: refused to level 0, and the fault handed on to the end (`exception_check`) | Exception out of `may_hold` |
| A thread's and a region's handle, with the rest of the first row | Thread, Region out of `may_hold` |
| `space_map` goes by the target space's level: a supervisor at 4 places a level-2 VMO in a level-2 space; a level-0 writer's refused | the caller's level instead |
| A region takes no process of another level, binds no space of another level | each test off |
| A space of another level is not cloned | the test off |

All through one job tree on every boot, so none is deactivated. `test-container` and
`test-cgroups` are unchanged, since everything there is one level.
