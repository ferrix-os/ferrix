**Rows, no claim:** `test-btrfs`, `test-btrfsd-restart` and `test-netd` run with `--memory 2048`: on the rows' default machine the promises and the drivers' fixed 64 MiB leave too little, so a further partition's 40,000 pages are short and `test-btrfs`'s root does not unpack. Unexplained. A next sighting captures the seal's report line: the first question is whether a refusal surfaces as `Fault` rather than `NO_MEMORY`.

**The once-in-eight (F.3):** a planted request read as served. Believed to be the compiler dropping a pair of `alloc::alloc` calls, since the check now calls the allocator itself, and 16 boots (`mbl-g10f3-0` to `15`) read none; at the old rate 16 clean boots occur about 12% of the time by chance, so that is no proof.

**What it costs, roughly:** `test-rustc`'s warm row read about 5.5 s sealed and 5.9 s unsealed, one debug run each under KVM on a busy host, so no measurable cost and no claim; bench-rows on release builds is the comparison's.

**The seal is the global allocator's alone:** page tables, stacks, IOMMU tables and `vmap` are the buddy's, and each report line says how many frames are outside every promise; those are not sealed, and the hard count check is a precondition of `built=yes` (F.8). A job with a promise is held to it; a job with none is refused, its nearest ancestor's being its own by `Quota::draws_from`; only a task with no job reaches the pool, whose use each report says: 6 frames of 4,096 in every row run so far (`test-rustc`, `test-btrfsd-restart`, `test-btrfs`, `test-net`, `test-netd`, `test-partitions`, `test-vfs`, `test-shell`), so 16 MiB is generous and not yet a bound. `mm::seal::check` holds these at each such boot. F.3's once-in-eight was the compiler dropping `alloc::alloc`'s pair, answering not null, so that the plant passed or failed by inlining: it now calls the allocator itself.

**Default, K16.2:** a safety partition's kernel objects are made at its start and it allocates nothing after READY; the bound and time are T1's. **Stated:** the reservation tag is a u8 (`MOST` 64), a byte a frame, and K12's one level stands, with a job's children drawing on its entry by copying its value at the slot's making. **Preconditions of `built=yes`, BACKLOG rows:** K16.5's static check of FATAL-ALLOC, K16.7's residue and refused restart, K16.9's table, F.8's count check.

**Customer, 2026-11-04: `max` keeps per-job heaps as its stated, justified deviation**, which is this design; K16.1's "stronger form" is withdrawn as a question. **The strict form, no allocator after boot, belongs to `max_plus` alone** (and for `btrfsd` too), which arrives with PR #38 and is not on develop. It attaches here as a further value of the flag, `kernel_memory=none`, whose allocator refuses after the seal with no routing, `mm::seal`'s `route` and pool compiled out and `mm::seal::check` then asking that all three kinds of request are refused; nothing in `retyped` assumes it is the last value.

## 1. What there is

K12 gives a job a **reservation**: frames off the allocator's free count, in entries tagged per frame (`DRAWN`,
page-exact), and K12b a **slab heap per reservation**, so a reserved job's kernel objects are on pages of its own
reservation (§2.4). F-23 made every allocation fallible; F-37 charges them. The global allocator stays: a job with
no reservation, and the kernel's own tables after boot, draw from one shared heap, `KernelAllocator`.

## 2. The rule, and its evidence

**After the seal, the shared heap refuses every request.** `KernelAllocator::alloc` under `retyped` serves a job from its
own promise, a task with no job from the pool, and otherwise answers null, once `seal_after_boot()` has run. Dynamic objects
remain, per job: the customer's answer of 2026-11-04 for `max`, its stated deviation. F-23's fallible paths surface a
refusal as `NO_MEMORY`. Three things must be true for the kernel still to work:

1. **Every job has a reservation.** The kernel makes a first job's at start, sized by the description for safety
   partitions and by a boot-sized share for `linux` and `drivers` (§4).
2. **Every kernel object an allocation makes names a job**, the one that pays (`quota::charge_target()`, K7's rule).
   `alloc::Vec` and `Arc` in the object layer go through `fallible::*`, and those route through **a current
   reservation**, thread-local to the task: `KernelAllocator::alloc` under `retyped` asks
   `reservation::heap_of(charge_target())`, the K12b slab heap. So no call site changes: a *global allocator* is linked,
   it **refuses every request not on behalf of a reserved job after boot**, and what it serves comes from that job's
   retyped pages. No request has no job: a task of the kernel's own (reaper, timer, RCU-like grace) has none, and each
   is either made not to allocate (§3) or given the kernel's boot-sized pool, stated by name.
3. **The boot's structures are sized at boot and never grow** (§3).

Retype: K12's entry is untyped memory, `DRAWN` its tag. **On creation a frame is retyped to one slab class of one
job's heap, and on the class's last free it returns to untyped.** K12's slab lists are per class per reservation; that
is the "per-type" list K12 §4 names. A frame retyped stays in that job's entry; no object crosses jobs: an object a
job's call makes for another (K7's ticket) draws on the ticket's job, already.

**Revocation.** seL4 revokes by capability derivation tree. Here a job's end frees its objects by the ordinary drop
path, each object's `Charge` naming the slab it is on; the entry returns when `drawn` is zero. Objects that outlive
their job (a VMO a handle in another job keeps, F-37's rule) **keep the dead job's entry alive** until they go: K12 §2.5
already does this. Bounding it is K16.7's, a precondition of `built=yes`.

**Nesting** (K12 §4, one level today): a child's reservation is carved from its parent's entry. `RESERVE` on the job
handle. Frames carved stay in the parent's accounting.

## 3. What allocates with no job

The list is the design's core: `check-fallible-alloc.py --report` over core and item under `retyped` names 41 unmarked
and 86 fatal-by-design today. Each falls in one bucket:

| Bucket | Example | Under `retyped` |
|---|---|---|
| boot only | `FATAL-ALLOC: boot only`, `DEVICES.call_once`, the root job | before the seal: unchanged; a post-seal reach panics in checks (the seal's control) |
| per-object, a job pays | handle tables, channel messages, VMO maps, `AddressSpace`, ports, tasks | the job's slab |
| per-CPU or per-boot tables | run queues, per-CPU records | sized at boot; a `Vec` that grows is a finding, and each becomes a fixed array or a boot-sized slab |
| kernel tasks | the reaper's lists, the audit ring, IPI lists | fixed rings; the reaper takes its list off the dying object |
| growing globals | `process::TABLE` (pid table), `CLAIMS`, `BINDINGS` (not built under user), the sleeper sets | sized at boot from the description's `max_processes`, a field of it (K16.9), and refused past it |

**The audit (K16.3, the first landing; ledger 6226 A.1-A.7).** By file, with who pays and the count of its sites, in
`tools/common/data/kernel-alloc-audit.json`. A site is what `check-fallible-alloc.py` flags and also the frame allocator's calls, page tables, kernel stacks, IOMMU tables and `vmap`.
`check-kernel-alloc-audit.py` fails on a file that allocates and is not listed, on a listed one that does not, and on a count that moved, so a new site is unread until read. A row's bucket is its worst site's. 69 files: 23 boot, 30 a job pays, 5 the kernel pool, 5 the allocators themselves, 5 with every site marked NOALLOC, 1 with no job.

It is a map that says where to look, and proves nothing. It reads source: a call it does not name is not seen. Each bucket is one reading of the file, one line in the JSON, not traced call by call. **Read:** `arch/x86_64`, `arch/aarch64` and `arch/arm_common`. **Unread, named on every run:** `arch/armv7a`, out of the claim, and `platform/`: under this shape M6 leaves its board glue out (`check-kernel-shape.py`'s LEFT) and K8b's generic publication stands in, so the AArch64 claim stands on none of the glue; what `platform/` still builds is 88 lines with no allocation read, and a safety board that needs glue joins this audit (ledger 6803). No "no shared pool" claim stands on this. K16.3's evidence, the count check and its control, is a BACKLOG row and a precondition of the seal going on.

Findings, each a BACKLOG row:

* **`object/mod.rs` `defer`**, traced: it grows `ORPHANS`, shared, from any close, falling back to a drop in place and then to abandoning the object. It has no job to charge. Under `retyped` the list becomes a link in the object.
* **Growing globals (K16.9, L.object.339).** `process::TABLE`, `user/address_wait.rs`'s shared table (`grow`, `shrink`), `object/quota.rs`'s slot chunks.
* **The kernel pool (§4) is not sized.** Its users: a domain's IOMMU tables, per bind; a kernel stack's virtual range; the slot chunks.

`arch/x86_64/apic.rs` was a finding and is closed: `quiesce_io_apics`' one caller is `apic::init`, at bring-up (A.6).

## 3a. What the review asks, folded (ledger 5942)

* **K16.1.** Phase one: no shared heap, and per-job dynamic allocation remains, bounded, a justified deviation. The row in MICROKERNEL, its line 656 and §6.5's table, and AoU-5 are rewritten to say that, AoU-5 amended and not retired. The stronger form is a morning question.
* **K16.2.** Owed, a choice: a safety partition allocates nothing after READY, or its allocations have a stated bound and time. The alloc path's lock hold and refill are bounded either way.
* **K16.3.** Above: frames as well as heap.
* **K16.4.** Allocation in an interrupt, a softirq or a kernel task is refused and listed, not routed, because `charge_target` there is whoever was running. Panic and machine-check paths allocate nothing. The kernel pool has a name, a size and a refusal: not yet.
* **K16.5.** Every FATAL-ALLOC is to be shown boot-only: a post-seal null reaching one panics the product. Check and control owed.
* **K16.6.** A job without a carve draws on its nearest ancestor's entry, through a per-task cached pointer, never a parent walk under a lock. `reservation::MOST` is 64 and the tag a u8: the width and its bytes per frame are to be stated, and K12 §2.6's one level amended. The allocator routes by reservation, kept right across moves.
* **K16.7.** Revocation: a safety partition's objects cross to no QM holder, or a kernel-side close at its death; residue stays carved from the parent, counted; a restart that does not fit refuses, named, with a control. §2's "charged to the parent" is withdrawn.
* **K16.8.** A frame retypes to a size class of one job, not per type: a deviation from K12 §4, stated. Frames zeroed on return to untyped before a user sees them, with a check. * **K16.9.** `max_processes` is a field of the description; no boot word sets a table's size. Coded in the format, read by no table yet.
* **K16.10.** `kernel_memory=retyped` requires `personality=user` in the resolver, with tests both ways; `max` does not build, so its rows are a no-claim `--set` build, labelled. L.object.336, 337 need FIRED controls.

## 4. Who gets what

A `max` machine's memory after boot: kernel boot-sized structures, each safety partition's reservation from its
description (K12's admission, §2.3), then `drivers` and `linux` split the rest by the description's ratio, or by a default
stated in `SAFETY-MANUAL`. **A QM partition cannot take what a safety one was promised**, K12's row, now true of the kernel
heap too, since there is no shared one.

Quotas stay: F-35 and F-37's limits apply *inside* a reservation, as today (K12 §2.6).

## 5. Rules and requirements

**H.MEM.32**: *after the seal the kernel's global allocator serves no request except from the retyped pages of the job
the request is charged to, and refuses every other.*

**H.MEM.33**: *the boot's structures are sized at boot and never grow.*

| Id | Rule | Check | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| L.object.336 | a post-seal allocation with no reservation behind it is refused | stage 9 of a `retyped` kernel: a task with no job allocates, null | **the row's own:** a planted allocation after boot on a shared path succeeds under the control's edit: *a planted allocation after the seal was served* |
| L.object.337 | an object is on a page of its job's reservation | `object.reserve_check`'s case, in all objects K12b's covers | the slab lookup answers another job's: *an object on another job's page* |
| L.object.340 | a retyped frame returns to untyped on its class's last free | counts | -- |
| L.object.341 | a child's reservation comes from its parent's, and its dead child's entry counts on the parent | case | -- |
| L.object.338 | every kernel task's allocation is on the table of §3 | `mm::seal_check`: a kernel task's request is charged to nobody, and the pool serves it | a planted one |
| L.object.339 | boot-sized tables never grow | a walk of the named globals' capacity at the seal and at the last check | a push past it: *a boot-sized table grew* |

## 6. Not now

`fast` keeps `heap`. AoU-5's dynamic-memory justification is retired for `max` only. Fragmentation inside a reservation is a
per-job matter, stated. No-allocation on the fast path (`fast_path_check`) unchanged.
