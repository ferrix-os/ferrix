## 1. A server's time charged to the client

K7's ticket (`object::ticket`, on `feature/mk-base`) follows a request: `issue_ticket`,
adoption by the serving thread, valid until the reply, and its slot is what memory is
charged to. **Time follows the same ticket.** While a thread has adopted a live ticket:

* **What moves is `quota`'s CPU charge and `cpu.stat`**: `sched::vtime` and `quota::switched`
  charge the ticket's job for the thread's CPU time, and the server's own job for none of it, so
  `cpu.max` throttles the **client's** job for a server's work on its behalf.
* **What does not** (6208 (2)): the thread's `vruntime`, which the fair queue reads to place it,
  stays the thread's, with its own priority and placement. This is accounting, not donation of a
  scheduling context. seL4's MCS donation moves the budget too; here a budget lives in a job and
  the charge is what moves. With HardRt in place, K9's HardRt row replaces the charge with the
  budget's own CBS, against the same ticket.
* **Under the queue lock** (6208 (3)) accounting reads one atomic on the task: the adopted ticket's
  quota slot, zero for none. No membership lock, no `Arc` dropped, no walk of parents. K7 checks a
  ticket only at a native call's entry, so that word is what ends the charge: **cleared by the
  reply, by adoption of the next ticket, and at the ticket's bound, whichever comes first**, and
  a job's end leaves a slot with no budget, whose charge lands in nothing, counted, and is never read as
  another job's: slots are not reused while a task names one.
* A server throttled by a client's quota would hold its other clients up. So the throttle of
  an adopting thread takes effect **at the ticket's end**, by the debt going to the client's job
  then: `throttle_current` skips a thread with an adopted ticket, and the debt stays on the
  client's job's account, which throttles the client's own threads at their next way out.
  So one client's overrun never stalls the server: the row's interference claim holds.
* **The skip cannot be an escape** (6208 (1)). T1 issues a ticket and blocks, so it cannot throttle;
  T2 adopts and spins, and is skipped as long as the ticket lives. So: **past the ticket's bound the
  skip ends and the adopter throttles as its own job**; and there is **no skip** for a ticket issued
  by the adopter's own job, or by a job with no `cpu.max` while the adopter's has one.
* A higher level's budget is never spent in lower-level code unless declared (§2.5), and K17 has no
  such declaration for a ticket, so (6208 (4)): a ticket whose issuer's level is above the adopter's
  job's **charges the adopter's own job**, and is counted, until K17 has a call that says otherwise.

**What holds the slot** (6300 U3). `Ticket::charge` is `quota::Charge::to`, which holds its slot;
the adopter's `Arc` keeps the ticket until the word is cleared under the queue's lock, so the slot
outlives every charge to it, even with the job gone. A case has the job go mid-spin.

**What the walks cost** (6300 U2). `cpu_capped` and `past_ticket_bound` walk the quota tree's parents on
an adopter's way out, with interrupts masked on the x86-64 path. The depth has no default cap
(`cgroup.max.depth` is `max` unless set), so the bound is the tree's depth as a job's own `cpu.max`
walk (`charge_bandwidth`) already has it, under the queue's lock, on every tick. Nothing here is worse
than that; neither is bounded by a constant.

**Two capped jobs on each other's tickets** (6300 U4) each move their time to the other, up to twice the
other's allowance a period. So a pair can run at most their own allowances plus each other's twice over,
and no more: the cap's bound is per pair, not per job, and this is stated rather than prevented.

**A bound.** A ticket charges up to twice its issuer's allowance in a period: the allowance and
one allowance's debt. Past that the charge lands on the server's job, which throttles as itself,
and a counter says so. Debt lasts the period, since `cpu.max`'s usage does.

## 2. The static time-partition schedule

`schedule=windows` (`BUILD-FLAGS.md`, `max`): a compile-time build of the scheduler, not a boot
option. A **major frame** of fixed windows per processor, ARINC 653's shape, each naming one
partition's job, set once at boot by the partition manager and never changed:

* `JOB_SET_WINDOW(job, *window)`, `SET_LIMIT` on the job, before the frame is sealed, `window` =
  `{cpu, offset_ns, length_ns}`; `FRAME_SEAL(root, major_ns)`, **the root job's handle alone** for both
  (6208 (8)). The seal checks that windows do not overlap on a processor and fit, and starts the frame
  on the next timer. A second seal, or a window after one, is `BAD_STATE`; a window on a processor out of
  service refused; all audited. Each a native number of its own (6208 (5)). **A `max` boot with no
  seal starts no partition below the manager**: fail closed.
* **The table is sized at boot** and the pick is a compare against the current window, with one absolute
  epoch per frame to find it (6208 (7)): bounded, and no drift.
* **At a window's start** the processor's tick goes to the frame (`CpuQueue::arm_timer`'s deadline
  takes the minimum of the fair slice and the window's end). In a window, `choose_next` picks only
  runnable tasks of jobs at or beneath that window's job; with none, **idle**, never another
  partition's: the slack is not given away (a time partition's point). Kernel threads run at their
  own reserved window, sized by the integrator, which is **stated as an assumption of use** and
  whose minimum the seal checks, with the reaper and the grace period's worker named.
* **At a window's end** a task running is preempted at the next instruction boundary that interrupts
  allow, with the overrun measured (the kernel's non-preemptible sections, `FX-0503`'s bound), not
  claimed. Interrupts stay delivered in any window (their time is the row's third half, unbuilt);
  device handling that wakes a task of another partition only queues it.
* An IPI for a wake across partitions is not sent: a wake queues and the partition's window sees it,
  which closes one timing channel §2.5 names. TLB shootdowns still interrupt: named, argued in T1.
* **Every other door asks too** (6208 (6)): `direct::hand_over` across partitions declines and goes the
  long way, and wake placement, balance, steal, push, `move_onto` and yield all ask the current window.
  This is to be redone against #38's `Stack` and #40's domains as they stand, before code.
* **Kernel sleep locks across a window's end** (6208 (8)) are named in the design that follows, and
  bound or argued: a holder preempted at a window's end keeps its waiters until its next window.
* **Inside a window**, fixed-priority preemption: the customer's C6 and C3 (2026-10-27, `docs/backlog/DECISIONS.md`),
  corrected here. An earlier line of this document had EDF or the fair queue inside a window; §4 is what stands.

**In the row's gate**: M3's "K9 interference row": a QM job at full load does not push a budgeted job
past its deadline, with a control that removes the budget. Measured on nazuna's KVM as a *development*
figure; no timing claim on it (`MICROKERNEL.md` §6.5).

**Compile-time shape.** `cfg(ferrix_schedule = "windows")` selects the pick and the arm; other profiles
carry none of it. **The kernel half is held** (6208 (9)): it lands with a boot that prints `windows`, either
a booting `max` or `--set schedule=windows` on a bootable profile, the second the quicker, brought as a
`BUILD-FLAGS.md` change. `ferrix_sched::frame`, the pure core, lands once something names it as a caller.

## 2a. `schedule=edf` (ledger 7011 S.4-S.8, 7088 E.1-E.5, G.1-G.3)

`fast`'s schedule: `ferrix-sched-edf`, a pure crate (no unsafe, no `ferrix_` cfg, no allocation after construction),
core ring, linked only when `schedule=edf`. **PR #38 holds no EDF**, and nothing here forks it: it holds the class
trait this crate would be re-homed under, and whoever lands #38 redoes the seam.

* **The seam (G.1).** `CpuQueue.fair` is `sched::Tier<Arc<Task>>`: `RunQueue<T>` for `fair`, `ferrix_sched_edf::OverFair<T>`
  for `edf`, the same code for `present` and `desktop`, in one alias. Every door a reservation meets is a method here:

| Method | `OverFair` answers |
|---|---|
| `enqueue` | a reserved id to its entry, under the wake rule; any other to the fair class |
| `pick_next` | puts back what runs, then earliest deadline with budget, then the fair class |
| `hand_over` | the fair class's, unless a reservation is here at all: then the long way, as `enqueue`, `remove_curr`, `pick_next`. `direct::begin`, the only way in, declines first (E.5) |
| `update_curr` | charges a running budget, throttling at zero; else the fair class's |
| `yield_curr` | gives up the budget until the next period; else the fair class's |
| `decision_in_ns` | the least of a running budget's end, the next refill, and the fair class's: so a refill wakes an idle processor |
| `should_preempt` | a running reservation out of budget or with an earlier one ready; else any reservation ready, or the fair class's |
| `remaining_ns`, `remove_curr`, `current`, `current_id` | the running reservation's, else the fair class's |
| `remove` | a ready reservation's parts; its entry stays, and until it comes back it is the fair class's wherever it goes (a processor out of service, below) |
| `len`, `is_empty`, `queued` | the fair class's plus the reservations on this queue, a throttled one counted: `direct`'s `waiting` declines on any |
| `latest_where` | **the fair class's alone**: steal, balance and push see no reservation |
| `for_each`, `get`, `avg_vruntime`, `load_weight`, `set_weight`, `set_request`, `slice_ns`, `set_slice_ns`, `config`, `level` | **the fair class's alone: stubs for a reservation, on purpose.** A reservation has no weight or lag; `measure`, `request_sample` and the load average don't see it. `direct`'s fold (T12, `JobPlan`) never runs with one here, which `begin` shows. |

  One test set runs on the crate; the fair class's own stands. (G.1's generic set over all three tiers is owed with `windows`.)
* **Admission (E.1).** Runtime at least 0.1 ms, deadline **equal to** period, period 1 ms to 10 s: summing `runtime / period` does not
  suffice for a shorter deadline. At most 95% and 32 entries a processor, the first with room, the caller's first.
  `EINVAL` through linuxd for the malformed, `EBUSY` for the unschedulable, as Linux answers.
* **Authority (E.2).** `THREAD_RESERVE(job, runtime, deadline, period)`, 0x10E1: on a job handle with `SET_LIMIT` at or above the caller's
  job, refused otherwise, so a native program or a second partition can't take 95% by asking. linuxd adds `CAP_SYS_NICE`.
  Calling thread alone; another thread's is `EPERM`. Three zeros give it back. Without `schedule=edf`, `NOT_SUPPORTED`: on `present` 0x10E1
  moves from no such call to that answer, as any new number does.
* **Life (E.3).** Pinned on admission: `set_affinity` answers `Reserved` (`EBUSY`). The share comes back when the task is freed, wherever
  it stands; `used` reads zero after the check's threads go. A child starts fair. Stopped or frozen, it leaves the queue
  and its budget and deadline wait under the wake rule. A move to another job leaves it: the budget is the thread's, `cpu.max` the job's. Given back
  while running, it stays the running entry until its next way off the processor, and never enters the fair tree while it runs,
  where a steal would find it. A processor taken out of service moves it with the rest: it runs fair where it lands, and its share stays
  counted until it ends or gives it back: stated, not re-admitted.
* **Locks (E.4).** A hard throttle of a thread holding a kernel sleep lock or linuxd's arena lock stalls everyone behind it until the
  refill, with no inheritance. A reserved thread that waits on `btrfsd` or a driver waits on the fair class's time. Named, not bounded: not owed for `built`.
  A throttled thread's kill or end waits for its refill, at most 10 s, the longest period. The audit records the attempt, refused or not.
* **Wake paths (E.5).** A refill reaches the timer with nothing running (`decision_in_ns`). A reserved wake with an earlier deadline preempts
  (`should_preempt`, and `wake`'s own `queue.should_preempt()`). The fast path declines at `direct::begin`.
* **Charge.** `runtime` and `cpu.stat` as today. A job's `cpu.max` still charges a reserved thread, acted on at its next way out. linuxd's own
  `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` does not see a throttle: BACKLOG. **Walk:** a scan of 32 entries under the queue lock, `MEMORY-AND-TIMING` 2.2.
* **Rows (S.6).** Host: `ferrix-sched-edf`'s tests. Boot: `reserve_check`, the `edf` line, kernel threads through the same `reserve::take`. Program:
  `test-reserve`. `test-shell`, `test-threads --smp 2`, `test-flags` under it. No timing claim, and stage 14's Exit stays Still to do (S.7).

## 3. Checks and controls

The charge: stage 9's `tickets` line gains a case: a server thread spinning on an adopted ticket charges
the client's `cpu.stat`, and the server's stays flat; control: the charge stays with the running job.
A throttled client's server keeps answering another client; control: the throttle applies to the adopter.
The schedule: host tests of `ferrix_sched::frame` under Miri (overlap, seal, pick), and in a `max` boot a
line `windows`: two jobs with windows of 2 ms and 3 ms, each runs only inside its own by its own stamps,
idle when empty; control: the pick ignores the window.

Added by 6208 (11): the escape of §1: a job with `cpu.max` whose thread adopts its own job's ticket and
spins is throttled, and past the bound any adopter is; control: the skip without those two tests. The
hand-over declining across partitions, and each refusal of §2's calls. `present` measured by B10's method
(`docs/microkernel/K1.md`), since accounting is on the switch path.

## 4. `schedule=windows`, as built (ledger 7011 S.1-S.3, S.6; 7088 W.1-W.4, G.1-G.3)

`max`'s schedule. The customer's C3 (no fair tier in `max`) and C6 (fixed priorities inside a window), `docs/backlog/DECISIONS.md`.
This section replaces §2 where they differ; §2's frame stands.

* **The crate.** `ferrix-sched-windows`: pure (no unsafe, no `ferrix_` cfg, no allocation after construction), host tests and
  Miri, core ring, linked only under `schedule=windows`. It holds the **frame** (per processor, at most 16 windows, each
  `{partition, offset_ns, length_ns}`, one `major_ns` for every processor, one absolute epoch) and the **class**, `Windows<T>`,
  which `sched::Tier` names under the flag as it names `RunQueue` and `OverFair`. **No `RunQueue` in it** (C3): a task is
  queued in an ordered set of the slots it already carries (`ferrix_sched::Ordered`, a public face of the run queue's AVL tree
  keyed by one word), one set per partition, so a pick in a window is the leftmost of one set.
* **Priority (C6, 7460 WN.9-WN.12).** Fixed, **from the task's nice, never a weight**: 40 levels, a lower nice first. The
  key is `(level, sequence)` compared plainly, never by the tree's wrapping `before` (WN.5): `Ordered` takes its own key;
  `sequence` is 56 bits, one per enqueue, which at 10^9 a second wraps in over two years of one queue, stated. **Round robin
  among equals with a quantum of 2 ms** (two of Linux's 1 ms RR intervals at HZ=1000 inside a 9 ms window: four turns a
  window, said), counted in running time only; **a quantum's remainder carries across a window's end** (the task resumes
  with what it had left at its next window). At a quantum's end the running task goes behind its equals; a higher level ready
  preempts at once. A lower level waits while a higher one is runnable in its partition: that is fixed priority, stated.
  Weights, lag and vruntime are not read: `follow_group_share`, `effective_weight`'s share and job weights are compiled out
  under the flag, and a `cpu.weight` write is refused by name (`EINVAL` through linuxd), a case. One fixed-priority
  round-robin body, `Fixed`, a type of its own over `Ordered`, which `Windows` composes per partition, so a later rr crate
  reuses it (TR.10). **The servers sit above the programs (WN.10):** native processes of the image (the manager, devmgr, the
  drivers, btrfsd, linuxd) take levels 0-9; linuxd maps a Linux program's nice -20..19 into levels 10-39, so a nice -20
  spinner under linuxd starves no server: a case. `sched_setscheduler`/`sched_setattr` under windows: `SCHED_OTHER`,
  `SCHED_BATCH` and `SCHED_IDLE` accepted as nice, `SCHED_FIFO`, `SCHED_RR` and `SCHED_DEADLINE` refused `EPERM`, said.
* **Partitions (W.1, W.2).** Partition 0 is the root job's: **every kernel thread and every task with no job runs in partition 0
  alone**, and nothing else does (W.2's one answer; "kernel workers above them" withdrawn). A job has a partition number, a byte,
  copied from its parent at its making and never changed. `JOB_SET_WINDOW` sets it, refuses a job with a thread or a child
  (`BAD_STATE`), refuses a job not a child of the root's, and refuses after the seal: so no parent walk and no move across
  partitions. A task takes its process's job's byte at its making, a plain field read under the queue's lock. A process moved
  to a job of another partition is refused (`BAD_STATE`), so the byte stays true.
* **No native call (7460 WN.1, WN.2).** The frame is **the kernel's policy, a constant**, `built`'s default frame below, not
  an integrator's. The boot authority sets it with the bodies the boot check calls (`frame::set_window`, `frame::seal`), on the
  root job by the kernel's own reference, before it starts the manager; no process holds the root job (BOOT-AUTHORITY §3), so no
  number is wired: 0x10E2 and 0x10E3 stay reserved, unassigned, and answer as any unassigned number under every schedule value,
  a case. The seal refuses overlap, a window past `major_ns`, a window on a processor out of service, a processor in service
  with no partition-0 window of at least 500 us (the floor: the reaper's and grace worker's one pass each, read at about 40 us
  apiece on KVM, with room for the timer's work; a stated figure, not a bound), and a second seal; each a boot-check case
  through the real body. **Unsealed, the boot authority starts no first process** and stops the boot (`FX-0927`): fail closed.
  Where an integrator's frame comes from (the sealed description through the kernel's bounded parser, or a manager with a new
  right) is a BACKLOG row bound to M7 and R.3, a design to review then.
* **A processor back in service after the seal** takes its row of the sealed table; a processor with no row (none in service
  at the seal) is refused its return, said, a case. Taken out of service, its row stays and is unused; its tasks move with
  their byte.
* **The default frame (W.4).** 10 ms on each processor in service: partition 0 from 0 to 1 ms, partition 1 from 1 to 10 ms.
  The manager, devmgr, the drivers, btrfsd and linuxd with its programs are all partition 1: the three first jobs are made in
  it. A safety partition gets its own number when an image has one; none does (R.3), so the manager splitting its window is a
  BACKLOG row, not built. Kernel threads (the reaper, the grace period's worker, work queues, the idle balancer) have 1 ms in
  10: **a grace period and a reap can wait up to 9 ms more**, stated (W.3). Interrupts are taken in any window. **The cost
  (WN.3):** partition 0's millisecond idles when its threads have nothing, so a tenth of every processor is given away. Every
  partition-1 path that waits on a partition-0 worker, each up to 9 ms more, is listed in §4.1. The boot authority runs in
  partition 0: its waits (the manager's READY, §4's watch) are counted in frames.
* **The pick.** The window is found from `(now - epoch) % major_ns` against the processor's table, a compare per window (at
  most 16). In a window, only the current partition's set is read; empty, **idle**, never another's. `decision_in_ns` is the
  least of the window's end and the running task's quantum. At the window's end the running task is put back and the next
  partition's set is read; **the overrun**, the time from the window's end to that pick, is measured, its worst per processor
  printed, not bounded by a claim (FX-0503's sections).
* **Before the seal** there is no frame: the class reads every partition's set as one, by level (the leftmost of at most
  `PARTITIONS` = 8 sets, fixed at construction, nothing allocated after: O(8) a pick, in MEMORY-AND-TIMING beside the windowed
  pick's O(16) compare and one tree operation), so the boot and its checks run as a fixed-priority machine; the seal turns the
  frame on at the next tick. A frame is a value; the boot's is one, kept in a `Once`, and the check's is its own, sealed through
  the same body and its refusals (so it carries a partition-0 window of the floor), installed on one processor the check holds,
  kept out of steal, balance, push and wake placement while it is on, and taken off before the boot's seal; taking a frame off
  is refused once the boot's is sealed, a case. Its stamps show nothing else ran in the two windows. Under the flag every boot
  check that judges fair shares, weights, `cpu.weight`, lag or slices is listed in §4.1 and compiled out with the reason or
  rewritten for fixed priority (WN.8); the boot line counts what was left out.
* **Wakes (W.4).** A wake into the current window's partition kicks as today. A wake of a task of another partition only
  queues it: no IPI, no preemption; the window's own timer finds it. A case shows both.
* **Every other door (6208 (6)).** `direct::hand_over` across partitions declines (`begin`), and within one goes the long way
  (as `edf`); steal, balance, push and the wake's pull take only a task whose partition is the destination's current one, and
  `latest_where` answers within one partition; `move_onto` and a processor out of service move tasks with their byte; yield
  goes behind its equals.
* **Sleep locks across a window's end (W.3).** A holder preempted at its window's end keeps its waiters until its next window:
  a waiter of another partition waits up to `major_ns` minus the holder's window, plus the holder's hold. Named: the kernel's
  sleep locks a task of partition 1 holds while partition 0's workers wait on it are the VMO and address-space locks
  (`user::space`), the channel's, the job tree's; none is bounded for `built`; the bound is owed at M7, a BACKLOG row (ledger 10087 A5) that FLIP.md's sorted table names. linuxd's arena lock is one partition's.
* **Under the queue lock.** One compare per window and one tree operation: `MEMORY-AND-TIMING` 2.2.
