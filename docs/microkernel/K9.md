(1)-(11), ledger 6208 (internal, not independent verification, F-27); §1 may start with them
folded here, and §2's kernel half is held.** `docs/MICROKERNEL.md` §3, row K9. HardRt itself (partitioned EDF with CBS

is not here. Two halves are: **a server's time on a client's request charged to the
client**, and **the static time-partition schedule** of `schedule=windows`. The third

Ids (6208 (10)): time charging and windows are the scheduler's, so **H.SCHED.26-27** and
**L.sched.153-170**, reserved on `feature/mk-restricted-ids`, and panic code **FX-0927**. New native
calls, not second meanings of `JOB_SET_LIMIT` (6208 (5)): numbers from the same landing, §2.

## 1. A server's time charged to the client

K7's ticket (`object::ticket`, on `feature/mk-base`) follows a request: `issue_ticket`,
adoption by the serving thread, valid until the reply, and its slot is what memory is
charged to. **Time follows the same ticket.** While a thread has adopted a live ticket:

* **What moves is `quota`'s CPU charge and `cpu.stat`**: `sched::vtime` and `quota::switched`
  charge the ticket's job for the thread's CPU time, and the server's own job for none of it, so
  `cpu.max` throttles the **client's** job for a server's work on its behalf.
* **What does not** (6208 (2)): the thread's `vruntime`, which the fair queue reads to place it,
  stays the thread's, with its own priority and placement. This is accounting, not donation of a
  scheduling context. seL4's MCS donation moves the budget too; here a budget lives in a job and
  the charge is what moves. With HardRt in place, K9's HardRt row replaces the charge with the
  budget's own CBS, against the same ticket.
* **Under the queue lock** (6208 (3)) accounting reads one atomic on the task: the adopted ticket's
  quota slot, zero for none. No membership lock, no `Arc` dropped, no walk of parents. K7 checks a
  ticket only at a native call's entry, so that word is what ends the charge: **cleared by the
  reply, by adoption of the next ticket, and at the ticket's bound, whichever comes first**, and
  a job's end leaves a slot with no budget, whose charge lands in nothing, counted, and is never read as
  another job's: slots are not reused while a task names one.
* A server throttled by a client's quota would hold its other clients up. So the throttle of
  an adopting thread takes effect **at the ticket's end**, by the debt going to the client's job
  then: `throttle_current` skips a thread with an adopted ticket, and the debt stays on the
  client's job's account, which throttles the client's own threads at their next way out.
  So one client's overrun never stalls the server: the row's interference claim holds.
* **The skip cannot be an escape** (6208 (1)). T1 issues a ticket and blocks, so it cannot throttle;
  T2 adopts and spins, and is skipped as long as the ticket lives. So: **past the ticket's bound the
  skip ends and the adopter throttles as its own job**; and there is **no skip** for a ticket issued
  by the adopter's own job, or by a job with no `cpu.max` while the adopter's has one.
* A higher level's budget is never spent in lower-level code unless declared (§2.5), and K17 has no
  such declaration for a ticket, so (6208 (4)): a ticket whose issuer's level is above the adopter's
  job's **charges the adopter's own job**, and is counted, until K17 has a call that says otherwise.

**What holds the slot** (6300 U3). `Ticket::charge` is `quota::Charge::to`, which holds its slot;
the adopter's `Arc` keeps the ticket until the word is cleared under the queue's lock, so the slot
outlives every charge to it, even with the job gone. A case has the job go mid-spin.

**What the walks cost** (6300 U2). `cpu_capped` and `past_ticket_bound` walk the quota tree's parents on
an adopter's way out, with interrupts masked on the x86-64 path. The depth has no default cap
(`cgroup.max.depth` is `max` unless set), so the bound is the tree's depth as a job's own `cpu.max`
walk (`charge_bandwidth`) already has it, under the queue's lock, on every tick. Nothing here is worse
than that; neither is bounded by a constant.

**Two capped jobs on each other's tickets** (6300 U4) each move their time to the other, up to twice the
other's allowance a period. So a pair can run at most their own allowances plus each other's twice over,
and no more: the cap's bound is per pair, not per job, and this is stated rather than prevented.

**A bound.** A ticket charges up to twice its issuer's allowance in a period: the allowance and
one allowance's debt. Past that the charge lands on the server's job, which throttles as itself,
and a counter says so. Debt lasts the period, since `cpu.max`'s usage does.

## 2. The static time-partition schedule

`schedule=windows` (`BUILD-FLAGS.md`, `max`): a compile-time build of the scheduler, not a boot
option. A **major frame** of fixed windows per processor, ARINC 653's shape, each naming one
partition's job, set once at boot by the partition manager and never changed:

* `JOB_SET_WINDOW(job, *window)`, `SET_LIMIT` on the job, before the frame is sealed, `window` =
  `{cpu, offset_ns, length_ns}`; `FRAME_SEAL(root, major_ns)`, **the root job's handle alone** for both
  (6208 (8)). The seal checks that windows do not overlap on a processor and fit, and starts the frame
  on the next timer. A second seal, or a window after one, is `BAD_STATE`; a window on a processor out of
  service refused; all audited. Each a native number of its own (6208 (5)). **A `max` boot with no
  seal starts no partition below the manager**: fail closed.
* **The table is sized at boot** and the pick is a compare against the current window, with one absolute
  epoch per frame to find it (6208 (7)): bounded, and no drift.
* **At a window's start** the processor's tick goes to the frame (`CpuQueue::arm_timer`'s deadline
  takes the minimum of the fair slice and the window's end). In a window, `choose_next` picks only
  runnable tasks of jobs at or beneath that window's job; with none, **idle**, never another
  partition's: the slack is not given away (a time partition's point). Kernel threads run at their
  own reserved window, sized by the integrator, which is **stated as an assumption of use** and
  whose minimum the seal checks, with the reaper and the grace period's worker named.
* **At a window's end** a task running is preempted at the next instruction boundary that interrupts
  allow, with the overrun measured (the kernel's non-preemptible sections, `FX-0503`'s bound), not
  claimed. Interrupts stay delivered in any window (their time is the row's third half, unbuilt);
  device handling that wakes a task of another partition only queues it.
* An IPI for a wake across partitions is not sent: a wake queues and the partition's window sees it,
  which closes one timing channel §2.5 names. TLB shootdowns still interrupt: named, argued in T1.
* **Every other door asks too** (6208 (6)): `direct::hand_over` across partitions declines and goes the
  long way, and wake placement, balance, steal, push, `move_onto` and yield all ask the current window.
  This is to be redone against #38's `Stack` and #40's domains as they stand, before code.
* **Kernel sleep locks across a window's end** (6208 (8)) are named in the design that follows, and
  bound or argued: a holder preempted at a window's end keeps its waiters until its next window.
* **Inside a window**, EDF or fair as the partition's job says; under `schedule=windows`, today's fair
  queue, until HardRt.

**In the row's gate**: M3's "K9 interference row": a QM job at full load does not push a budgeted job
past its deadline, with a control that removes the budget. Measured on nazuna's KVM as a *development*
figure; no timing claim on it (`MICROKERNEL.md` §6.5).

**Compile-time shape.** `cfg(ferrix_schedule = "windows")` selects the pick and the arm; other profiles
carry none of it. **The kernel half is held** (6208 (9)): it lands with a boot that prints `windows`, either
a booting `max` or `--set schedule=windows` on a bootable profile, the second the quicker, brought as a
`BUILD-FLAGS.md` change. `ferrix_sched::frame`, the pure core, lands once something names it as a caller.

## 3. Checks and controls

The charge: stage 9's `tickets` line gains a case: a server thread spinning on an adopted ticket charges
the client's `cpu.stat`, and the server's stays flat; control: the charge stays with the running job.
A throttled client's server keeps answering another client; control: the throttle applies to the adopter.
The schedule: host tests of `ferrix_sched::frame` under Miri (overlap, seal, pick), and in a `max` boot a
line `windows`: two jobs with windows of 2 ms and 3 ms, each runs only inside its own by its own stamps,
idle when empty; control: the pick ignores the window.

Added by 6208 (11): the escape of §1: a job with `cpu.max` whose thread adopts its own job's ticket and
spins is throttled, and past the bound any adopter is; control: the skip without those two tests. The
hand-over declining across partitions, and each refusal of §2's calls. `present` measured by B10's method
(`docs/microkernel/K1.md`), since accounting is on the switch path.
